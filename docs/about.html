About Silvio Fanzon — academic background, applied mathematics, modelling and professional transition

About

From mathematical research to applied modelling and decision tools.

I am an applied mathematician and statistician with a career spanning research, university teaching and programme leadership. I now bring that background to industry-facing work in data science, optimisation, operational research and scientific computing.

I have spent my career learning how to take difficult, ambiguous problems and make them precise: identify the right assumptions, build a defensible model, implement it computationally, test what it can and cannot say, and explain the result clearly.

My academic work ranges from optimisation algorithms and dynamic MRI reconstruction to statistical models for Formula 1 and curling. Alongside research, I have led degree programmes, designed data-science teaching, supervised projects and built technical resources for varied audiences.

Academic career

Research, teaching and leadership across three universities

2014–2018

University of Sussex · UK

EPSRC-funded PhD in Mathematics

Studied variational models, PDEs and material microstructures, graduating with a viva outcome of pass with no corrections. I also worked as an Associate Tutor across analysis, geometry, probability and statistics.

2018–2021

University of Graz · Austria

Postdoctoral Researcher

Worked on an Austrian Science Fund project in motion-aware medical imaging, developing mathematical frameworks and optimisation algorithms for dynamic inverse problems under severe noise and undersampling.

2022–2023

University of Graz · Austria

Faculty member · University Assistant

Contributed to the Inverse Problems and Mathematical Imaging group through research, undergraduate and postgraduate teaching, supervision and grant-development activity.

2023–2026

University of Hull · UK

Permanent Lecturer in Applied Mathematics

This is the UK academic grade closest to Assistant Professor. I combined research with module leadership, PhD and dissertation supervision, and later served as Programme Director for the MSc in Mathematics.

What comes next

Carrying the strongest parts of academia into industry

In 2026 I chose to leave my permanent academic post and transition toward industry. That career remains the foundation of how I work: rigorous problem formulation, evidence-based reasoning, long-horizon project ownership and the ability to communicate technical ideas to different audiences.

I am particularly interested in roles where mathematical modelling, statistics and computation support prediction, operational decisions or scientific discovery.

International perspective
