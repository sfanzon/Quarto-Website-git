# Website narrative and portfolio strategy

## Positioning decision

The website now treats the academic career as a source of evidence rather than something to hide during the transition to industry.

The central narrative is:

> Applied mathematician and statistician who can frame difficult problems, build defensible models, implement them computationally, evaluate the evidence and communicate the result.

The academic career supports this narrative through an EPSRC-funded PhD, postdoctoral research, faculty appointments, a permanent UK Lecturer role (the grade closest to Assistant Professor), MSc programme leadership, supervision, publication and international collaboration.

## Page roles

- **Homepage:** concise positioning, five capability areas, three visible projects, selected publications and news, then a closing academic-background section.
- **About:** the full career narrative and the reason it matters to the next stage.
- **Expertise:** transferable capabilities and evidence, written for employers rather than as a research statement.
- **Research:** the three academic research themes, written accessibly but without diluting the mathematics.
- **Projects:** inspectable outputs demonstrating modelling, software, documentation, communication and leadership.

## Recommended next portfolio projects

These should not be advertised as finished until they are genuinely usable. In priority order:

### 1. Probabilistic forecasting and decision dashboard

Build an end-to-end forecasting product using a public time-series dataset such as energy demand, transport use or service volume.

Include:

- data ingestion and cleaning;
- strong naive and statistical baselines;
- rolling-origin backtesting;
- prediction intervals and calibration;
- scenario comparison and a simple decision recommendation;
- an interactive dashboard;
- reproducible environment, tests and concise technical documentation.

This adds forecasting, data engineering, evaluation, uncertainty communication and product thinking—the largest gaps in the current public portfolio.

### 2. Operational optimisation decision tool

Build a small but realistic scheduling, allocation or capacity-planning application.

Include:

- a clear stakeholder problem;
- objective and operational constraints;
- a mathematical optimisation model;
- feasible baseline heuristics;
- scenario and sensitivity analysis;
- service/cost trade-offs shown in a lightweight interface;
- documentation written for a non-mathematical decision-maker.

This would make the operational-research positioning concrete rather than merely stated.

### 3. Turn the Formula 1 research into a data product

The paper and code already provide a strong base. Convert them into a polished, maintained application with:

- automated data preparation;
- reproducible model fitting;
- driver-versus-constructor comparisons;
- uncertainty and model-diagnostic views;
- an interactive explainer;
- a concise API or downloadable results;
- continuous integration and a clear project architecture.

This is likely the fastest route to a portfolio project that shows statistics, software, communication and user-centred design together.

### 4. Modernise the DGCG implementation as research software

Turn the existing implementation into an inspectable software project with a package structure, reproducible examples, benchmark cases, tests, API documentation and a short non-specialist explanation of the problem and results.

This would demonstrate software engineering without abandoning the strongest technical research.

### 5. Publish the Statistical Models materials as a coherent learning product

The material already demonstrates communication and curriculum design. Strengthen it with a clear learning path, reproducible notebooks, short datasets, exercises with solutions, accessibility checks and a public repository that shows how the content is maintained.

## Recommended future homepage projects

Once the first two new projects are complete, the strongest three-project homepage combination would be:

1. probabilistic forecasting and decision dashboard;
2. operational optimisation tool;
3. Formula 1 analytics product.

The MRI/DGCG work should remain prominent on the Research and Projects pages as evidence of depth, while the homepage would then show a broader industry-facing range.
